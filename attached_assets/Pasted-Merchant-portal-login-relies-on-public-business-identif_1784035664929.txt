Merchant portal login relies on public business identifiers and has no brute-force protection
High
Merchant Authentication and Approval
An attacker can sign in to a merchant account using only the business's commercial registration number and phone number, with no password, one-time code, or other secret. Because the login endpoint also lacks any rate limiting or lockout, attackers can repeatedly guess phone numbers for a known business until they take over the merchant portal.

The merchant login flow in artifacts/api-server/src/routes/auth.ts accepts only crNumber and phone from the request body, looks up a merchant row with eq(merchantsTable.crNumber, crNumber) and eq(merchantsTable.phone, phone), and immediately creates an authenticated session with req.session.merchantId = merchant.id. There is no password hash, OTP challenge, possession check, or approval gate anywhere in this flow. The client code in artifacts/lailtak-merchant/src/context/AuthContext.tsx and artifacts/lailtak-merchant/src/pages/Login.tsx confirms that these two fields are the entire login ceremony. lib/db/src/schema/merchants.ts likewise stores only profile fields such as phone, email, and crNumber; there is no authentication secret to verify.

This is especially dangerous because crNumber is a public business identifier by design, and the remaining factor is just a phone number, which is often public-facing business contact data or can be brute-forced from a small search space. The application has no evidence of rate limiting, backoff, CAPTCHA, or account lockout on /api/auth/merchant/login, so an internet attacker can script repeated login attempts until one succeeds. Once authenticated, the attacker receives a server session cookie and can access all merchant-only endpoints guarded by req.session.merchantId, including profile data, dashboard metrics, booking queues, and venue management routes under /api/merchants/:id/*. This enables full merchant account takeover rather than mere enumeration.
